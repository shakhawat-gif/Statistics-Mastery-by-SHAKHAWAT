The word "Statistics" comes from Latin "status" — meaning STATE.

It started as literally "the science of the state" — governments collecting numbers for taxation and military conscription.

Achenwall coined "Statistik" in 1749.

**Tweet 3:**
Quick history you should actually know:

• John Graunt (1662) — founding father of statistical demography
• Galton — regression & correlation
• Karl Pearson — chi-square, correlation coefficient
• R.A. Fisher — ANOVA, MLE, hypothesis testing as we know it today

**Tweet 4:**
Statistics has TWO senses, and conflating them causes confusion:

1) Plural sense → the actual DATA ("the statistics show...")
2) Singular sense → the METHOD (collect → organize → analyze → interpret → present)

You need both to not be misled by numbers.

**Tweet 5:**
Here's the trap question: "Statistics proves things." True or false?

FALSE.

Statistics deals in probability and likelihood, not proof. A significant result doesn't "prove" a hypothesis — it tells you the data would be unlikely under the null.

**Tweet 6:**
Limitations nobody tells you on day 1:

• Deals with AGGREGATES, not individuals (ecological fallacy risk)
• Statistical "laws" are true ON AVERAGE, not universally
• Can absolutely be misused/misinterpreted — this is a HUMAN failure, not a math failure
