**Tweet 2:**
First trap: "variable" ≠ "variate."

Variable = the concept/container (e.g. "height")
Variate = a specific realized value, when treated as a random outcome (e.g. "172cm")

Most people use these interchangeably. In probability theory, the distinction is real.

**Tweet 3:**
Second trap: "mean" ≠ "average."

"Average" is the lazy umbrella term — could mean mean, median, OR mode.
"Mean" is one specific calculation: sum ÷ count.

Every mean is an average. Not every average is a mean.

**Tweet 4:**
Third trap (this one gets people in vivas):

Is a population parameter (μ) random?

NO — in frequentist stats, μ is fixed but unknown. What's random is your SAMPLE STATISTIC (x̄), because it changes every time you draw a new sample.

**Tweet 5:**
Mediator vs Moderator vs Confounder — three words people swap constantly:

• Mediator: explains HOW (sits in the causal chain)
• Moderator: explains WHEN/for WHOM (changes relationship strength)
• Confounder: creates a FALSE relationship (causes both X and Y)
